# Agency Proposal Scoping and Commercial Clarity

A proposal should translate a validated business problem into activities, deliverables, responsibilities, timing, and measurement. A catalogue of every agency service is not a scope.

## Recommended proposal structure
1. Prospect context and confirmed problem.
2. Desired outcome and measurement approach, including baseline limitations.
3. Included services and explicit exclusions.
4. Deliverables with quantities or acceptance criteria where agreed.
5. Agency and client responsibilities.
6. Timeline, dependencies, approvals, and access requirements.
7. Agency fee, media spend, third-party tools, and taxes/fees where applicable, shown distinctly.
8. Assumptions, risks, change-control process, and terms.
9. Start conditions and next step.

## Scope example
Vague request: "We need to grow on Instagram."
Business outcome: "We need more qualified consultation requests."
Potential scope to validate: audience and message review, content plan, agreed creative production, call-to-action path, lead capture, and reporting. Volumes and deadlines must be agreed—not invented by the assistant.

## Pricing integrity
Do not provide a firm quote before understanding scope, complexity, delivery capacity, and external costs. If sharing an indicative range, label it as conditional and state the assumptions. Keep media spend separate from agency fees.

## Pre-send checklist
Confirm the problem in the prospect's own terms; connect each activity to an outcome; define deliverables; clarify client dependencies; separate third-party spend; remove unsupported guarantees; version and date the proposal.
