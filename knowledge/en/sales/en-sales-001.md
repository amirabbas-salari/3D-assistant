# Agency Lead Qualification Framework

## Purpose
The first conversation is for understanding the prospect's business problem, checking initial fit, and agreeing on a useful next step—not for pitching every agency service.

## Minimum discovery context
Capture the business model, primary offer, target customer, geography, current acquisition channels, stated challenge, desired business outcome, previous attempts, timeline, decision participants, and budget status. Record unknowns as unknown; never manufacture a budget or deadline.

## Qualification dimensions
- Need fit: Is the stated problem within the agency's actual capabilities?
- Business fit: Does the account match the agency's target market and delivery capacity?
- Budget fit: Is there a plausible investment range for the required scope?
- Authority: Are the relevant decision-makers involved or accessible?
- Timing: Is there a concrete reason and realistic timeframe to act?

## Discovery sequence
Ask permission and set expectations; understand the business; explore the problem and its impact; ask what has already been tried; define a measurable desired outcome; clarify stakeholders and timing; then agree on a next step.

## Evidence-based qualification
A lead should not become qualified merely because the prospect is enthusiastic. Record the problem, evidence of fit, expected outcome, constraints, unresolved questions, and next action.

## Agency-specific caution
A full-service agency may offer SEO, paid media, social content, creative, web, analytics, and CRM services. Do not assume the prospect needs all of them. Recommend only services connected to a validated business problem and delivery capacity.

## Honest selling
Do not guarantee rankings, revenue, lead volume, or ROAS without a defensible basis and contractually defined conditions. Explain dependencies such as offer quality, sales response, site conversion, seasonality, and media budget.
